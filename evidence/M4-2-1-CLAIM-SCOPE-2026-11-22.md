## 1. Narrowed claim

M4.2.1 is reported as **Implemented, not real-market verified**. The validator
resolves an expected schedule from checked-in calendar artifacts and rejects
bars outside that schedule. Those artifacts are test fixtures. The gate
establishes internal consistency of a registration against a declared fixture,
not the validity of an exchange calendar, the origin of a dataset, the
completeness of a source, or the correctness of a trading result.

No real-market calendar validator is claimed. No source completeness is
claimed. No profitability claim is made. Pure mathematical checks over scalars
and abstract timestamps are outside this claim and assert no market observation

## 2. Review findings

F1. A fixture calendar marked `synthetic:false` can certify `MARKET` origin.

A calendar artifact under `research/python/fixtures/calendars/` can carry a
`synthetic:false` declaration. The registry resolves that artifact by identifier
and recomputes its digest. Neither operation establishes who published the
calendar or whether it matches an exchange session schedule. A checked-in
artifact with that flag can therefore satisfy a registration that states
`MARKET` origin.

Required provenance states are `TEST_FIXTURE`, `EXCHANGE_PUBLISHED`,
`PROVIDER_PUBLISHED` and `OWNER_VERIFIED`. A calendar provenance state must be
recorded for each artifact. A `TEST_FIXTURE` calendar must not satisfy market
acceptance under the dataset acceptance contract, independent of its boolean
flag or its digest.

F2. An expected schedule generated from observed first and last timestamps
cannot detect a missing first or last bar.

The expected schedule is the set of scheduled observation timestamps inside the
observed UTC-nanosecond range. If the first or last observation is absent from
the dataset, the range narrows to the observations that are present, so the
absent endpoint falls outside the range and no mismatch is raised. The gate
cannot detect truncation at either boundary.

Independent coverage bounds are required from the dataset manifest. The
manifest must state the declared coverage start and end before the validator
constructs the expected schedule, so a missing first or last bar is detected
against a declared boundary rather than against the data being checked.

F3. A bar-close anchor over an inclusive 09:15–15:30 session yields 376 bars,
against the 252 × 375 convention.

Inclusive counting of both 09:15 and 15:30 produces 376 timestamps in a regular
session. The named one-minute annualisation basis fixes 375 bars per session
and 94,500 periods per year (252 × 375). The current basis does not define how
a close-anchored schedule maps to the 375-bar count, so a close-anchored bar
series can disagree with the registered period count.

Bar-open and bar-close schedule math must be defined explicitly: the anchor,
the first and last scheduled timestamp, and the resulting bars per session must
be derived from a declared rule. The period count must be bound to that rule
and checked against the registration. Exchange sessions must not be inferred
from test fixtures or from raw timestamp differences.

## 3. Additional scope limits

Coverage statuses (`no_trade`, `not_listed`, `suspended`, `halted`,
`market_closed`, `missing`) are recorded as explicit observations with
`price=None` and `fabricated=false`. Those statuses are not authority-checked:
no exchange notice, surveillance list, issuer filing or published holiday
schedule is consulted to confirm them. A status observation records an
assertion; it is not an authoritative fact about a venue.

The search adapter does not pass status observations as an integrated workflow.
Status handling is exercised in the cadence and registry tests, not as a step
that carries a status observation through search registration, evaluation and
report output. No end-to-end status workflow is claimed.
